03 // OVERVIEW

ABSTRACT

Hannah Arendt's phrase “a society of laborers without labor” (1998:5) frames this project's exploration of youth unemployment and professional uncertainty under Sustainable Development Goal 8 (SDG 8).

The phrase raises a question for young creatives: how can people retain agency when the work around which they build their lives changes? As artificial intelligence transforms creative industries, uncertainty about employment can contribute to anxiety, disengagement and passive resignation. The Tang Ping ("lying flat") phenomenon provides a context for exploring these responses among young people (Ren et al. 2026). This connects Arendt's warning to SDG 8's commitment to productive employment and decent work for all.

Drawing upon the Daoist philosophy of Wu Wei, our theme explores the tension between passive resignation and intentional adaptation. We propose engaging with technological change without surrendering creative autonomy. The two p5.js generative artworks express interdependence through contrasting forms: folded volumes in one, and interwoven black and white shapes whose intersections become gray in the other. Their changing compositions give visual form to the relationship between structure, chance and adaptation.

Our Call to Action, “Grasp the Flow, Be Fluid,” invites young creatives to respond to the prospect of “a society of laborers without labor” by reclaiming their agency, integrating emerging technologies into their practices and actively shaping their professional futures.
